If you're someone who's worked in the tech industry (or any industry, really), you've probably borne witness to the many ways in which the products you've built (and organizations you've worked for) can be harmful.

Worse still, you've probably seen that harm be *intentional*: calculated decisions to prioritize profit over people. Chances are you've also watched the market respond to those decisions in the kind of way that gives other executives *exciting new business strategies*. If you're like me, you may have also encountered a Discord user with an anime avatar rubbing it in your face as an example of "how the *real world* works".

I won't go through an exhaustive list of _all_ the reasons I think this happens, but I believe that it's enabled the most by one thing we can--though rarely do--change:

## Corporate hierarchy

In the current corporate meta where executives and majority shareholders rule with little to no accountability, it's difficult for employees to do much to meaningfully influence an organizations direction beyond handing in their resignation. They have limited voting power or individual leverage (if any), bills and mortgages to pay, skills that have atrophied in environments where survival prefers doing the wrong work for the right people, and a job market that is widely regarded as *not great*.

Despite all this, some triumph and manage to steer the ship, but many still are forced to watch as their C-suite drowns Leonardo DiCaprio in the North Atlantic.

Network effects, walled-gardens, and lack of competition--due to years of ineffective and unenforced US antitrust law--make it difficult for consumers to vote with their wallets.

Over-representation of psychopathic traits in leadership roles coincide with low-empathy decision-making at the helm. A predatory corporate ecosystem selects for the most ruthless; a hands-off approach to regulation expects barbarians and thieves to reform once crowned king.

Absent pressure otherwise, company diversity in the US (and abroad) is largely determined by *survival of the shittest*.

Excessive layers of middle-management provide a way for the layers between to absolve themselves of any responsibility for their involvement--casting blame and aspersions at the feet of their corporate overlords--while simultaneously sharing in the spoils of their misdeeds.

Even with the best intentions, the pressure to perform and meet expectations can lead to decisions that prioritize local profits over global well-being.

Maximizing growth above all has led to a pretty large tab for humanity to pay, with the ones ordering fancy cocktails and multiple entrees conveniently sitting it out in the restroom.

## The solution

Abolish the hierarchy.

Or, at least, the kind that exists in most companies today.

Create flat (_or other interestingly-shaped_) organizations to add checks and balances on power. Build communities where every member has a say, where decisions are made collaboratively rather than top-down. Let experts in their domains make decisions they're best equipped to make, and defer to others for those they're not. Incentivize participation, discourage apathy, punish exploitation. Care for others to know you'll be cared for.

Reject deals that force you to invest into an unfavorable power dynamic just because they're considered the norm. Don't accept the notion that starting a company or being an early investor is a valid reason to sit above others. Stop idolizing wealth and excess. Save your attention for something meaningful.

Be transparent. Be accountable. Be different. Be brave.

Don't be evil.
